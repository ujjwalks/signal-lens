# Step 5 — Name the lead, which is often not the poster

Check `seller.motion` first; `dual` changes the answer for most signals.

- **The poster** — the default.
- **The employer, not the individual** — where a company pays for what a person needs.
  They post; someone else signs. This is what `motion: dual` means.
- **A third party in the thread** — the me-too commenter who volunteers their own scale
  under someone else's rant. A thread is a container of leads, not one lead.
- **The person who inherits it** — on a continuity break, the lead is whoever picks up
  the workaround, not whoever is leaving.
- **The channel, not a buyer** — whoever keeps answering recommendation threads.
  Pitching them is the fastest way to lose them. Their own twin is the paid affiliate:
  a link, a coupon code, suspiciously consistent loyalty.
- **Nobody.** Some signals identify a company with no contactable person, and some a
  person you must not contact. **"No lead" is a valid outcome** — return it rather than
  manufacturing one.

Where the signal is an artifact rather than an utterance, the thing you watch and the
thing you contact are different: a review's lead is the business reviewed, or on a
software review site the reviewer's employer. **Never make the competitor the lead** —
their footprint moving is evidence about their customers, and if you cannot enumerate
those customers, say the signal is real and unusable.

In B2B name both roles from `seller.buyer` where they differ: the one who feels it and
the one who signs.
