# Step 4 — Give every signal a doppelgänger

A signal without a named doppelgänger is not finished, and the doppelgänger is
what the `false_positive` column in step 7 gets filled from.

Every signal has a twin that shares its vocabulary and inverts its meaning: the
supply-side actor, the student, the past-tense war story, the affiliate, the
vendor farming the thread, the person on the other side of the desk, the
post-purchase reassurance-seeker, the hobbyist who enjoys the manual work.

The reason this step is separate from step 3 is that you cannot see a signal's twin
while you are still pleased with having found the signal. Do the whole list, then
come back and try to break each one.

## The discriminators that keep working

- present tense vs past tense
- "we" plus a number vs "what do people generally use"
- a specific mechanic hit *this month* vs the general vibe of the category
- asking *how* to verify vs asserting verification is impossible
- a question mark at the end vs a screenshot at the end

## The one that catches people

In any community that is mostly practitioners, the highest-volume lookalike is a
**peer**, not a buyer: someone who does this for a living, discussing it with
others who do. They use the buyer's exact vocabulary and have no budget and no
intent. Where `seller.industry` names a professional field, assume the default
reader of any thread is a peer and say what distinguishes your buyer from them.
