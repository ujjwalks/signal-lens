# Signal library

A floor, not a ceiling. These recurred when the derivation was run across ten unrelated
businesses — B2B software, agencies, a local trade, DTC consumables and durables, a law
firm, telehealth, education. Walk them, keep what fits, and **add what this seller has that
is not here.** The additions are the valuable part.

Examples are real specimens from that run. Notice how little they sound like marketing.

## The mandatory qualifier

**Countable structure fact.** Not a signal type — a requirement on every signal.

Severity is invisible from language. *"This takes forever and I hate it"* is worth nothing
from a two-entity company and five figures from a fourteen-entity roll-up, and the number
that decides which almost never appears in the subject line. It shows up as a
parenthetical, in a comment three replies down, or inside a job description someone else
wrote.

Every signal must carry a count of the **right** noun — the one derived as artifact C.
Venting has adjectives; intent has integers.

## Arrangement-as-object

The highest-yield group, and the one a pain-shaped frame cannot see. These are about the
product or arrangement breaking — dated, checkable, cohort-forming, safe to act on.

### Clock on the incumbent arrangement — 9/10

No third party is demanding anything. The existing arrangement simply has an expiry, and
the buyer's own words contain the date. Renewal window with terms disclosed, trial or
return-window countdown, replenishment, coverage cliff, stipend expiry, contract end.

**Watch the inverse twin:** *"ran out two weeks ago and I feel exactly the same"* is a
churn signal, not a repurchase signal. The buyer ran a natural experiment and the product
lost.

### Continuity break — the person carrying the workaround is gone — 8/10

> "She built the spreadsheet, she's leaving in three weeks, and nobody else knows how it
> works. There is a tab called DO NOT TOUCH and I don't know what it does."

**The inheritor is the highest-quality lead shape in the corpus:** they have the problem,
the access, and zero emotional attachment to the incumbent solution.

Require evidence of **sole** ownership — "no runbook", "nobody else knows". A team that
loses one of four maintainers is not a signal.

### Vendor death / orphaned installed base — 7/10

An incumbent EOLs, shuts down, is acquired and rebranded, loses a licence, retires, or
refuses a warranty — and thousands are stranded on a publicly announced date.

Distinguish an **announced EOL date** from acquisition anxiety. Most acquired products run
unchanged for two years and their customers correctly do nothing.

### Broadcast cohort shock — 9/10

The biggest structural addition, because it **changes the unit of work from post to
event → cohort.** A licence or pricing change, a recall or reformulation, a competitor
stockout, shrinkflation, a compliance date, a subsidy launching, a platform algorithm
shift, one lender imposing one template across a portfolio.

Detect once, enumerate the affected population independently, and work it with a public
date — rather than waiting to scrape one complaint at a time.

### Just churned off something — 9/10

The narrowest window and the highest intent. They have decided to leave and have not
decided where to go.

> "Returned the Autonomous on day 25, refund is pending. So I'm back to square one."

**Doppelgänger:** category exit, not brand switch. Someone who returned a standing desk
because they dislike standing is not in your market.

### Unhappy with what they use — 8/10

A complaint naming an incumbent from any of the three competitor tiers.

> "Fragomen has had our H-1B amendment for six weeks and our point of contact has changed
> three times. We're 40 people. I think we're just too small to matter to them."

**Doppelgänger:** the helpful commenter diagnosing someone *else's* problem. Identical
brand names, identical specifics, identical irritation, not in market.

## Buyer-in-motion

### Second opinion / pre-commitment validation — 10/10

**Present in all ten and absent from every draft frame.** The latest-stage signal in the
corpus and the cheapest to convert. They have one specific thing in hand — a quote, a
diagnosis, a fee agreement, a proposal, a loaded cart — and are asking strangers for
permission or refutation.

> "Got quoted $14,500 for EB1A plus government fees, premium processing extra, 50% up
> front. For reference the NIW quotes I collected were 7-9k. Is 14.5 crazy?"

**The discriminator is tense.** *"Wants $11,400"* is pre-decision. *"I paid $11,400"* is
post-purchase reassurance with nothing left to sell.
**The emotional tell:** *"am I being farmed"*, *"is this normal"*, *"talk me out of it"*,
*"what am I missing"*.

### Asking what it costs — 10/10

Someone with a quote in hand, or asking what people actually pay, has moved from wondering
to budgeting. **Doppelgänger:** the idle "how much does this cost" with no category chosen
and no number in hand — six months out, if ever. The tell is a specific figure.

### Asking for a recommendation — 10/10

The asker is the lead. The replies are enrichment: competitors named tells you who you are
against; nobody answering is an open door; you named is a warm intro — join, do not pitch.

**Doppelgänger:** the chronic researcher who has posted the same request three times in a
year.

### Counted failed attempt loop — 6/10

> "412 applications, three phone screens." · "Called 14 numbers, four disconnected."

The workaround failing **with an integer attached**, and the integer is what separates it
from venting. **Doppelgänger:** the same count from someone you cannot help — a senior
engineer with 400 applications does not need more school.

### Outgrown their setup — 8/10

The workaround breaking under load. **Doppelgänger:** the cheap fix. Half of these are
solved by a $180 part rather than a purchase.

### Doing it the hard way — 8/10, medium

Asking how to do manually what you sell. Strong on pain, weak on budget — many are doing it
by hand precisely because they will not pay. **Doppelgänger:** the committed DIY cohort,
where posting is a statement of identity rather than a request.

### Gatekeeper requirement you cannot meet — 7/10

A third party with veto power imposes a **spec** rather than a date: a lender's template, a
security review demanding data residency, a red tag from the gas utility, an RFE naming the
prongs, an RFP requirement.

Uniquely valuable because **the gatekeeper names the exact capability gap in their own
words** — which is a specification for the reply.

### Deadline imposed — 5/10

An external clock: a filing date, an audit, a compliance date. Where a computable date
exists this is the strongest signal available.

## Thread-shaped

### Me-too commenter — 7/10

**A thread is a container of leads, not one lead.** The peer who volunteers their own scale
under a generic rant, the "we're with them too" under a complaint, the "same boat,
following" under an ask.

These commenters **self-selected into a pain description someone else wrote**, which is
cleaner qualification than anything they would have authored themselves.

### The repeat recommender — 10/10, and not a lead

Found in every industry. Someone who answers the same question over and over in your
category.

**Lead: nobody.** They are distribution — one relationship worth more than one deal, and
pitching them is the fastest way to lose the channel.
**Doppelgänger:** affiliates. A link, a coupon code or suspiciously consistent loyalty means
paid.
**One exception:** where the seller has a multi-account SKU — firm plan, portfolio mandate,
employer seats — the same person is simultaneously the channel *and* the largest single
deal. Collapsing them into a one-seat prospect loses both.

## Conditional

| Signal | Applies when | Seen |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware the category exists** | The category is young, or the symptom is misattributed. *"Is there actual software that does this or does everyone just use Excel?"* The only uncontested volume in any market — and structurally invisible to anything matching against the seller's own site, because the post shares zero tokens with it | 6/10 |
| **New person in a buying role** | B2B, and the role owns a budget. New mandate, wants to change something in 90 days | 4/10 |
| **Building it themselves** | Buyer is technical. **Works on a lag** — key a timer to the second breakage. Approaching at the build announcement reliably produces a defensive "why would I ever pay for this" | 5/10 |
| **Hiring for the pain** | B2B only. Two leads at different times: the hiring manager now, the new hire on their start date | 6/10 |
| **Trigger event** | Only where money mechanically converts into your category within a quarter — see the funding note below | 7/10 |
| **Third-party-funded budget** | A stipend, rebate, tuition reimbursement or statutory payer exists, with rules and an expiry | — |
| **Institutional mirror** | Dual-motion sellers only: an employee's complaint is a lead for their employer, never for them | — |
| **Proxy purchase** | Household or gift purchases where user, payer and decider separate | — |

## Deleted, and why

Each of these was in an earlier draft and was cut on evidence.

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Has the problem"** (bare) | ~100% recall, ~1% precision — a topic filter, not a signal. Worse, it orders the market backwards: the most emotionally vivid posts skew to the smallest accounts. Keep only in qualified form — pain **plus** a count, an artifact, a date, or product awareness |
| **Public commitment** | 8/10 cut it. In B2B it is content marketing written for reach; the companies with a real plan say nothing until it is done. In B2C it is January resolution noise |
| **Promoted into authority** | 8/10 cut it. Internal promotion is the *cheap* option — the budget conversation already went the wrong way, and the promoted insider has less standing to demand tooling because they were promoted for succeeding manually |
| **Funding round** as a default trigger | Survives only because it is easy to detect. Maximally contested — every vendor emails the same list the same morning — and the need lags the raise by two to three quarters. Replace with the causal sub-events: an acquisition, an entity-count change, a regulated-market expansion, a sponsor takeover |
| **Comparing options** as vendor-vs-vendor | Keep the type, **derive the comparison object**. In three of ten, buyers compared delivery models (agency vs in-house vs freelancer), legal pathways, or equipment specs — never vendors |

## The artifacts that are not posts

A structural finding worth acting on: in at least four of the ten sellers, **the
highest-conviction artifacts are not posts at all.**

Job descriptions · published status-page RCAs · vendor sunset announcements · licence
changes · stale or reposted requisitions · inspection reports · lender templates · recall
notices · aged GitHub issues.

> An engine built only on post-shaped detectors over Reddit and LinkedIn will miss most of
> the actual value for a large share of sellers.

A two-year-old feature request with employer context attached is a higher-conviction
artifact than almost any post.
